# STELE

**The typed, sealed codec for agent-to-agent communication.**

When two AI agents communicate by passing natural-language strings, both sides pay encode and decode tax on every hop. The wire carries massive redundancy. Audit trails are unparseable for a regulator. Cross-cultural register is lost at translation. The substrate eventually hits its own bandwidth wall.

STELE is the structural answer.

## How it works

Twenty grammar atoms compose into glyphs at the BNF level. Five operators, five tense markers, six type primitives, four provenance markers. Every glyph carries a cryptographically sealed hallmark — when it was emitted, where, in what cognitive era, by which witness cohorts, certified by whom, inheriting from what parent. The same engine encodes prose into glyph and decodes glyph into any natural language at any cultural register.

The atoms are designed to be learnable by a transformer. Sparse, place-valent, compositional, low-fan-out. A glyph-native model trained on the corpus the codec produces is the v3.0 endpoint.

## Five surfaces, one engine

- Internal agent-to-agent — the dense traffic, thousands of events per day in production
- Agentic-to-human — decode-only, for human consumption
- Human-to-agentic — encode-only, for human input
- Human-to-human cross-cultural — wife on Earth writes English; husband on Mars receives Mandarin with Anglo-American 21st-century domestic register preserved end to end
- Machine-to-machine — substrate to external system, with full cryptographic time-anchoring

Compression target on internal traffic: four to six times in v1.0 hybrid notation, eight to fifteen in v2.x pure-symbol script. Audit-grade by construction. Per-event sealable for regulatory provenance.

## Why it matters

The same atom set composes the substrate's own protocol specification, making the codec self-describing in the same form it transports. A regulator inspecting an EU AI Act-regulated deployment cannot decode JSON soup buried in event logs. They need sealed, decodable, chain-walk-verifiable hops. STELE delivers Article 13 transparency, Article 14 oversight, and Article 15 robustness by construction, from day one.

Internally, the codec turns the dense agent-to-agent traffic that would otherwise blow context windows and burn API tokens into compact typed glyphs that an analyst, a regulator, and a glyph-native model can all read. The same artefact serves three purposes.
