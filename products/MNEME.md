# MNEME

**The typed memory substrate.**

MNEME is what Auryum's substrate runs on. It is the layer that decides what gets remembered, what gets forgotten, what gets predicted, and what gets watched.

Most AI systems treat memory as a logging problem. Write everything. Retrieve when asked. Hope the context window holds. MNEME treats memory as the foundational engineering problem of an autonomous reasoning substrate, with four typed layers built from the start.

## The four layers

**Episodic.** What happened, when, by which agent, with what outcome. Sealed via cryptographic chain. Every event verifiable end to end. A regulator querying *"what did AURELIUS conclude about Institution X on 14 March 2026?"* gets a sealed event with parent-link provenance, not a stack trace.

**Semantic.** What the substrate believes is true, with calibration history. Two-level Bayesian meta-confidence, derived from retrieval signal, drift signal, dependent prediction surprise, and counterfactual stability. Beliefs decay by tier — IDENTITY claims at five-year half-life, DRAFT claims at sixty days. Floor protections prevent demotion below the tier minimum.

**Predictive.** Forward-looking propositions in Friston discipline, with Tetlock-grade resolution criteria refused at write time if ambiguous. Every prediction carries a horizon band, a prior form, a posterior form, an actor, and a seal. Surprise is computed on resolution. The substrate gets calibrated against itself.

**Metacognitive.** The substrate's belief about its own labels. When MNEME's classifier mislabels a fact, the meta-confidence layer flags it. Adversarial label-flip audits run quarterly.

## Why it matters

An AI substrate without typed memory cannot maintain calibration across sessions. It cannot defend a claim against a regulator three years later. It cannot tell when its own beliefs are drifting. MNEME makes all three tractable, by construction, from day one.
