Most failure analysis is autopsy. Auryum is wind tunnel.

## The thesis

Institutions fail with structure. The structure repeats across civilisations. Roman senatorial dysfunction in 60 BCE has a measurable kinship with twenty-first-century corporate self-deception, with Soviet-era information cascades, with mediaeval guild collapse, with twenty-first-century social-media dissolution. Different surfaces. Same underlying mechanics.

The mechanics are typed. Specific gene-level dynamics fire in specific combinations. Specific frameworks of fragility activate when specific compounds are present. Specific genome profiles fail in specific characteristic ways.

For three thousand years this was readable only after the fact, and only by historians and economists working over decades. The civilisational record was too large for any single analyst, and the patterns crossed languages, eras, and disciplines that no individual could span.

Auryum is what becomes possible when AI agents can read the full record at once, in twenty-two civilisational dialects, against a typed institutional ontology, with cryptographic provenance on every claim.

The output is institutional fragility intelligence — sub-hour, calibrated, audit-defensible — for the sovereign clients (banks, hedge funds, defence primes, family offices, regulators) who today pay Forrester or McKinsey for slower static reports that cannot be defended in front of a regulator.

## How a case works

A sovereign client asks: *"should we be worried about Institution X?"*

The substrate runs the question through the case pipeline. ATLAS pulls the public corpus on the institution. SCRUTATOR runs cross-civilisational research against the question. COHORS reads the institution's behaviour through twenty-two civilisational dialects to surface convergent patterns. ARETE certifies the case against the substrate's accumulated calibration history. SEPULCRUM seals every artefact with a SHA-256 chain anchor and a Ghost Network RFC 3161 timestamp.

The customer receives, sub-hour:

- The institution's genome — which of the 43 institutional genes are active, at what intensity, with what supporting evidence
- The fragility score — calibrated probability of collapse on each of five horizon bands (nowcast, tactical, operational, strategic, civilisational)
- The collapse pathway — which of the 15 frameworks are firing, in what order, with what tipping points
- The evidence chain — every claim sourced, every source sealed, every seal verifiable offline
- The calibration history — Auryum's track record on similar cases, with Brier-grade scoring

Cases stay alive after delivery. ARGUS picks up that the subject was arrested last week. SCRUTATOR's corpus catches a new disclosure filing. The case updates in place. The customer asks once and stays informed.

The case library currently contains forty-four certified institutional cases. Subjects include FTX, Wirecard, and other public institutional failures — runs that produced both empirical validation of the framework set and live operational cases. The methodology is documented at [methodology/CASES.md](https://github.com/Auryum-Systems/.github/blob/main/methodology/CASES.md).

## Why most of this is private

Auryum operates a three-tier classification model. Public claims sit at this profile and at the website. Client-facing materials are released under NDA during sovereign engagements. Internal substrate (the specific gene IDs, framework names, compound names, genome profile identifiers, codec atoms, simulation templates, calibration tables) stays in vault — AWS S3 with KMS encryption — never in tracked source.

The seven repositories backing this organisation are private because the institutional-fragility ontology is the trade-secret content that gives Auryum's products their edge. Architectures are patentable. Lexicons are not. Both stay protected.
